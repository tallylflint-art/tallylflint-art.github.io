Is This Package Delivery Text a Scam? USPS, UPS, and FedEx Texts Explained

Short answer first

Delivery companies do not text you a payment link to release a package you are not expecting, and they do not ask you to "confirm" your address and card details through a link in a text. If you were not waiting for a package — or the text wants money or personal details — treat it as a scam and do not tap it.

How to tell the delivery text is fake

Look for this combination:

  • You are not expecting a package, or the details do not match. No order number you recognise, no item you ordered, a vague "your package" with no company name you can check.
  • A small fee unlocks the delivery. "Pay $1.50 redelivery fee," "customs charge due," a link to pay before the driver can come. Real delivery fees are not collected by surprise text link.
  • A link that asks for personal details. The page asks for your address, card number, or login "to verify." That page is harvesting your details, not tracking a parcel.
  • Odd sender and sloppy wording. A personal mobile number, a random email-style address, misspellings, or a shortened link that hides where it really goes.
  • Urgency about a deadline. "Your package will be returned tomorrow," "final notice." The rush is there so you tap before you check your real orders.

This wave is not fading. The Oct 25, 2026 AntiScam™ research says the toll / DMV / delivery text wave was still current in Oct 2026, and that the FTC ranked fake delivery texts the #1 text scam of 2024. The same research cites $470 million lost to text scams in the US in 2024, five times the 2019 figure (FTC Data Spotlight 2025, via secondary summary). A text that looks small is exactly why it works at scale.

What to do right now

  1. Do not tap the link. Do not enter an address, card, or login on the page it opens.
  2. Do not reply to the text — not even "STOP" or "who is this?" A reply tells the sender your number is live.
  3. Check your real orders another way. Open the shop's own app or website, or the delivery company's own site, and track there. Never use the link from the text.
  4. Forward the text to 7726 (that spells SPAM on most phones) to report it, then delete the text.
  5. If you already tapped and entered details, call your bank or card company right away, change any password you entered, and report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
