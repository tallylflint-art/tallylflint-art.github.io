Is This Text Message a Scam? How to Tell a Fake Delivery, Bank, or Toll Text

Short answer first

If you were not expecting it, and it wants you to tap a link, pay something, or reply in a hurry — treat it as a scam until you have proof it is real. Real delivery companies, banks, and toll agencies do not text you a payment link out of nowhere, and they do not threaten to lock your account or fine you by text.

How to tell it is a scam

Watch for these signs, especially together:

  • You were not expecting it. No package on the way, no toll road driven, no bank problem you knew about — yet the text says there is one.
  • A link you are pushed to tap. "Confirm your address here," "pay the fee here," "verify your account here." The link is where the theft happens — it leads to a fake page that steals your login or card details.
  • Urgency. "Pay today or your package is returned," "your account locks in 2 hours," "final notice." Real companies do not rush you by text.
  • It asks for money, a login, or personal details. A card number, a password, a code from your phone, or a gift card to "release" something. No real delivery or toll message asks for any of that.
  • The sender looks wrong. It comes from a random mobile number, a long string of digits, or an email address sending a text — not the company's own published number. Delivery, bank, and toll texts all use this same trick, so the name in the message means nothing on its own.
  • It is a toll or DMV text. Toll and "unpaid fine" texts are one of the most common text scams of 2026. A real toll agency does not collect fines by text link.

Fake delivery texts were the #1 text scam of 2024, and the wave was still current in Oct 2026. The pattern never changes: an everyday worry — a package, a bank account, a toll — plus a link, plus a hurry.

What to do right now

  1. Do not tap the link, and do not reply. Replying tells the scammer your number is live, and tapping opens the fake page. Even replying "STOP" can mark you as a real person.
  2. Do not pay, log in, or send personal details. Not through the text, not through any number in it.
  3. Check it yourself another way. If it claims to be a delivery, open the delivery company's own app or type their address yourself. If it claims to be your bank, call the number on the back of your card. Never use a number or link from the text.
  4. Forward the text to 7726, then delete it. 7726 (SPAM) reports it to your phone company. Save a screenshot first if you may report it.
  5. Report it. Report the text at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. If you already tapped and entered details, call your bank or card company right away and tell them it was a scam.
