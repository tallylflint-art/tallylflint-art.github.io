Check If a Phone Number Is Spam — Free Phone Number Spam Checker

Short answer first

A number you do not know, calling about a prize, a refund, a debt, or a package — or asking you to call back fast — is worth checking before you answer it or call it back. Different number every time is the trick: scammers change numbers so blocking one never stops them. Check the number first, then decide.

How to tell a number is spam

Watch for these signs, especially together:

  • One ring, then it hangs up. The caller wants you to call back — and calling back connects you to a premium-rate number that charges you by the minute. Never call back a number that rang once.
  • A number that looks local, but is not. Some spam calls use Caribbean area codes that look like US numbers, and some use a number with your own area code so you think it is a neighbor. A familiar-looking number is not proof it is a real person.
  • A premium or 900 number. If the number starts with 900, or the caller says to dial a number you have never seen to "claim" something, stop. Real companies do not make you pay to hear from them.
  • An international number you never called. A call or text from a country you have no connection to, about a prize, a refund, or a delivery, is spam.
  • A toll-free number pretending to be a company. Scammers put a real company's name on a toll-free number that is not the company's own published number. Look the company up yourself instead of trusting the caller ID.
  • What the caller says is the real giveaway. A refund you never asked for, a prize you never entered, a debt you do not owe, a threat about your bank account — or a request for gift cards, a code from your phone, or a wire transfer. Any of those settles it, whatever the number looks like.

One more thing to know: a scammer can fake the number on your caller ID, so the number on your screen may belong to a real person who never called you. That is called spoofing — the number alone can lie, which is why the pattern matters more than the digits.

What to do right now

  1. Do not answer a number you do not know, and do not call it back. Let it go to voicemail. A real caller will leave a message you can check.
  2. Do not give out codes, card numbers, or gift card numbers to any caller, no matter what they claim. No real bank, delivery company, or government office asks for those by phone.
  3. Block the number, then report it. Report spam calls at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. If you already paid or shared details, call your bank or card company right away and tell them it was a scam.
  4. Check the number free before you call back. If you are unsure, check it first — it takes less than a minute.
