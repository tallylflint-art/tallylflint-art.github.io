Is This Package Delivery Text a Scam? USPS, UPS, and FedEx Texts Explained

Short answer first

Almost always, yes. The Postal Service does not text you about a package unless you signed up for tracking alerts yourself first. And no real carrier — USPS, UPS, or FedEx — asks you to pay a small "redelivery fee" or "address correction fee" through a link in a text. That little fee is the hook: the page behind it is built to steal your card number and personal details. If a delivery text wants money or information through a link, it is a scam — do not tap it.

How to tell it is a scam

  • You never signed up for text alerts. USPS only sends package texts to people who asked for them. A delivery text out of nowhere — especially about a package you are not expecting — did not come from the Post Office.
  • A small fee unlocks the delivery. "Pay $1.99 redelivery fee," "customs charge due," "update your address to release your package." Real carriers do not collect surprise fees by text link, ever.
  • The link asks for your details. The page wants your address, card number, or login "to verify." That page is harvesting your information, not tracking a parcel.
  • The details do not match your real life. No order you recognise, no item you bought, just a vague "your package" — because the same text went to thousands of people, and it only needs a few to bite.
  • Odd sender, sloppy wording. A personal mobile number or a random email-style sender, misspellings, or a shortened link that hides where it really goes.
  • A deadline rush. "Your package will be returned tomorrow," "final notice." The hurry is there so you tap before you check your real orders.

What to do right now

  1. Do not tap the link. Do not enter an address, card, or login on any page it opens.
  2. Do not reply to the text — not even "STOP." A reply tells the sender your number is live.
  3. Check your package the safe way. Type the carrier's official site or app in yourself — usps.com, ups.com, or fedex.com — or open the shop's own app, and track it there. Never use the link from the text.
  4. Report it. Forward the text to 7726 (that spells SPAM on most phones), then delete it. You can also report the scam at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
  5. If you already tapped and entered details, call your bank or card company right away, change any password you used on that page, and watch your accounts closely.
