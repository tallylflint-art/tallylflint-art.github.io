Is This Job Offer a Scam? How to Tell a Fake Job From a Real One

Short answer first

If the job found you by text or message, pays a lot for very little work, interviews you only by chat, and asks you to deposit a check, buy equipment, or send money or ID early — it is a scam. Real employers do not hire by surprise text, do not send you a check to shop with, and do not ask for your bank details or ID before a real hiring process. Employment scams were the #2 riskiest scam reported to the BBB in 2025.

How to tell it is a scam

  • You never applied. A recruiter texts or messages you about a job you never looked for, often "remote," "flexible," and strangely well paid.
  • The pay is too good for the work. Hundreds a day for simple tasks, liking videos, rating products, or reshipping packages. Easy money is the bait.
  • The interview is only by text or chat app. No real office, no video call with a real person, no company email address — just fast messages and pressure to start today.
  • They send you a check. A check to buy equipment or supplies, with instructions to send part of the money on. The check is fake; it bounces after your real money is gone. This is the heart of most job scams.
  • They ask for ID or bank details up front. Your Social Security number, ID photos, or bank login "for payroll" before any real paperwork — that is identity theft, not hiring.
  • You have to pay to start. Training fees, starter kits, or crypto "task deposits." A real job pays you; you never pay it.

What to do right now

  1. Stop and do not deposit any check they sent. If you already deposited one, tell your bank right away that it may be fake.
  2. Check the company yourself. Search the company name plus the word "scam." Go to the company's real website by typing its address yourself, and look for the job there. If it is not listed, it is not real.
  3. Do not send ID, bank details, or money. Not for equipment, not for training, not to "unlock" earnings.
  4. Report it. Report the scam at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. If you shared your Social Security number, consider a credit freeze with the credit bureaus.
