Is This Lottery Win or Prize Text a Scam? How to Tell Before You Pay Anything

Short answer first

If you did not enter a lottery or sweepstakes, you did not win it. And a real prize never asks you to pay a fee, a tax, or a delivery charge to receive it. If the message asks for money first, that is the giveaway — no matter how official the letter, text, or call sounds.

How to tell it is a scam

Watch for these signs, especially together:

  • You never entered. Real drawings only pay people who bought a ticket or entered. A win you never entered is not a win.
  • A fee comes before the prize. "Pay $5 to release it," "cover the tax first," "send a gift card to confirm" — that fee IS the scam. The prize never arrives, and the fee is gone.
  • Pressure to hurry. "Claim in 24 hours or lose it," "only 3 winners left," a countdown clock. Real prize offices do not rush you into paying.
  • Payment by gift card, wire, or crypto. No real lottery asks to be paid in gift cards or cryptocurrency. That request alone settles it.
  • A stranger's contact details. The message comes from a personal email, a mobile number, or a messaging app — not the lottery's own published office — and it asks you to keep the win secret.

The Oct 25, 2026 AntiScam™ research notes older adults are more likely than younger adults to lose to prize and sweepstakes scams, and cites a live Oct 2026 example: postcards promising $309.64 in vouchers, then a $5 fee followed by a $46 charge. Small first fees are how the bigger loss starts.

What to do right now

  1. Do not pay anything, and do not send gift card codes. Not even a small "processing fee" to test it.
  2. Do not tap links or call numbers in the message. Look up the lottery or company yourself, from a number you find on your own, if you want to check.
  3. Do not reply, and do not share personal details — no bank details, no ID numbers, no address "to deliver the prize."
  4. Save the message, then delete it. Keep a screenshot if you may report it, but do not keep tapping it.
  5. Report it. Forward a scam text to 7726, and report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. If you already paid, call your bank or card company right away and tell them it was a scam.
