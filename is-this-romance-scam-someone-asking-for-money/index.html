Is This a Romance Scam? Someone You Met Online Is Asking for Money

Short answer first

If someone you met online — and have never met in person — asks you for money, gift cards, or cryptocurrency, that is a romance scam. It does not matter how kind they sound or how long you have been talking. A real love interest does not need you to pay their emergency bills, and a real emergency is never solved with a gift card.

How to tell it is a scam

Watch for these signs, especially together:

  • Love comes fast. Big declarations — "you are the one," talk of marriage or a future together — within days or weeks of meeting online. The rush is the setup.
  • They can never meet you. Always a reason: working overseas, in the military, on an oil rig, stuck at a border. Video calls keep failing, or they refuse them. Someone real who loves you finds a way to be seen.
  • Then comes the emergency. A hospital bill, a sick child, a frozen bank account, a plane ticket home, a package held at customs — always urgent, always needing your money right now.
  • The payment method gives it away. Gift cards, wire transfer, or cryptocurrency. No real person in an emergency asks to be paid in gift cards. That request alone settles it.
  • The story keeps changing. Details shift, dates do not add up, and questions get dodged. When you catch one lie, another story appears.
  • They ask you to keep it secret. "Do not tell your family, they would not understand." Secrecy is how the scam survives — because the people who love you would see it.
  • Their profile looks too perfect. A model-quality photo, a job that sounds impressive but vague, a profile that is very new. The photo is often stolen from a real person's social media.

This is not something to be embarrassed about. Romance scams work because they use real feelings — loneliness, hope, kindness — against good people. The shame belongs to the scammer, never to the person who cared.

What to do right now

  1. Stop sending money — and do not send more to "get back" what you already sent. Every new payment is a new loss. There is no fee that unlocks a refund of the first payment.
  2. Do not send gift card codes, wire transfers, or crypto, and do not open a bank account or move money for them. Moving money for someone you met online can make you part of a crime without you knowing it.
  3. Save everything. Keep the messages, the profile, the photos, and any payment receipts. Take screenshots before anything disappears.
  4. Report the profile to the dating app, social site, or messaging app where you met them, so they cannot do it to the next person.
  5. Report it. Report the scam at ReportFraud.ftc.gov and to the FBI at ic3.gov. If you sent money, call your bank or card company right away and tell them it was a scam.
  6. Tell someone you trust. A family member, a friend — saying it out loud breaks the secrecy the scam needs. Our team can also look at their messages with you, without judgment.
