Is This QR Code a Scam? How to Check a QR Code Before You Scan It

Short answer first

A QR code is just a hidden web address. If you did not expect it, if it is a sticker placed over another code, or if scanning it opens a page that asks for payment, a login, or personal details — stop. Do not pay and do not sign in. Scammers stick fake codes over real ones on parking meters, chargers, menus, and signs, and send fake codes by email and on package slips, because you cannot see where the code really goes until you have already scanned it.

How to tell it is a scam

  • It is a sticker on top of another code. Run a finger over it. A raised edge or a second code underneath means someone covered the real one. Peel-back stickers on parking meters and payment signs are a classic.
  • It arrived by email, text, or on a package slip. "Scan to confirm your delivery," "scan to avoid a fine," "scan to see your refund." Real delivery and government messages do not make you scan a code to fix a problem.
  • The page it opens asks for card details or a login right away. A menu code should open a menu. A parking code should open the city's known payment app — not a strange page asking for your full card number and password.
  • The web address looks wrong. After scanning, your phone shows the address before the page loads or at the top of the browser. Misspellings, extra words, or a company name inside a longer strange address are red flags.
  • It rushes you. A fine, a locked account, or a lost package if you do not scan and pay now. Rush is the scammer's best tool.

What to do right now

  1. Preview before you open. Most phone cameras show the web address after scanning, before opening it. Read it. If it looks wrong, do not open it.
  2. Never pay or sign in on a page a surprise code opened. Close it. Go to the real service yourself — the parking app you already have, the company's own website typed in by hand, or pay at the meter or desk another way.
  3. If you already paid through a fake code page, call your bank or card company right away and tell them it was a scam. Change any password you typed in.
  4. Report it. Report the scam at ReportFraud.ftc.gov, and tell the business or city whose sign was covered — you may save the next person.
