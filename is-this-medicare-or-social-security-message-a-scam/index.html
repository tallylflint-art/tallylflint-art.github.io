Is This Medicare or Social Security Message a Scam? How to Tell

Short answer first

Almost always, yes. Medicare and the Social Security Administration do not call, text, or email you out of the blue to ask for your Medicare number, your Social Security number, or a payment. They do not threaten to cut off your benefits over the phone, and they never ask to be paid by gift card, wire transfer, or cryptocurrency. If a message does any of that, it is a scam — hang up or delete it.

How to tell it is a scam

  • It asks for your Medicare or Social Security number. Scammers want these numbers to bill Medicare in your name or steal your identity. A real agency that already has your number does not phone you to ask for it.
  • It says your benefits will stop. "Your Medicare will be cancelled," "your Social Security payment is suspended." Fear is the tool. Real benefit letters come by mail and give you time and a real way to appeal.
  • It offers a new card, a benefit increase, or a free benefit for a fee. A "new plastic Medicare card," a "benefits review," extra money back — for a payment or your bank details first. Medicare cards are free and are never sold by phone.
  • It rushes you. "Act today," "stay on the line," "do not hang up or tell anyone." Real agencies do not rush you and never tell you to keep a call secret.
  • The caller ID looks official. Numbers can be faked (spoofed) so the screen shows a real agency name. The name on the screen proves nothing.

What to do right now

  1. Hang up. Do not press any buttons, and do not call the number back from the message.
  2. Call the real number yourself. For Social Security, call 1-800-772-1213. For Medicare, call 1-800-633-4227 (1-800-MEDICARE). Type or dial it yourself — never use a number from the suspicious message.
  3. Never pay by gift card, wire, or crypto for anything to do with your benefits. No real agency accepts them.
  4. Report it. Report the scam at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. If you already gave out your Social Security number, also watch your Medicare statements and bank accounts closely and tell your bank.
