Is This IRS or Tax Message a Scam? How to Tell

Short answer first

Almost always, yes. The IRS does not text you, email you, message you on social media, or call you demanding immediate payment. Real IRS notices about a refund or a tax bill come by mail — a letter you can hold in your hand. The IRS never takes payment by gift card, wire transfer, or cryptocurrency, and it never threatens to arrest you or deport you over the phone. If a message does any of that, it is a scam — hang up or delete it.

How to tell it is a scam

  • It arrived by text, email, or social media. The IRS does not start a conversation about your taxes that way. A "refund pending — tap to claim" text is fake, every time.
  • It demands payment right now. "Pay today or face arrest." Real tax bills come by mail, give you time, and give you a real way to ask questions or appeal.
  • It wants gift cards, wire transfers, or crypto. No government agency is ever paid that way. The moment you hear "gift card," the conversation is over — it is a scam.
  • It asks for your bank details to "release" your refund. The IRS already has the information from your tax return. A link asking you to type in your bank account or Social Security number is stealing, not refunding.
  • It threatens you. Arrest, a lawsuit, losing your driver's license, losing your benefits — fear is the tool. The IRS does not threaten first and explain later.
  • The caller ID says "IRS." Numbers and names can be faked (spoofed). The name on the screen proves nothing.

What to do right now

  1. Hang up. Do not tap the link, and do not call the number in the message back.
  2. Check your real tax record yourself. Go to IRS.gov — type it in yourself, never through a link from the message — and use the official "Where's My Refund" tool, or call the IRS at 1-800-829-1040.
  3. Never pay a tax bill by gift card, wire, or crypto. If you truly owe, the IRS gives you normal ways to pay, explained in a mailed letter.
  4. Report it. Forward a scam IRS email to reportphishing@irs.gov, and report the scam at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. If it threatens you or someone could be in danger, call your local police. If you already paid or gave out your Social Security number, tell your bank right away and watch your accounts closely.
