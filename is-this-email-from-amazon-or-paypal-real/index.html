Is This Email From Amazon or PayPal Real? How to Spot a Fake Brand Email

Short answer first

Real Amazon and PayPal emails never ask you to log in through a link in the email, never ask for your password or a code from your phone, and never threaten to close your account in a few hours. If the email pushes you to click and sign in, stop — and check it another way before you touch it. The name in the "from" line means nothing on its own: anyone can put Amazon or PayPal there.

How to tell it is a scam

Watch for these signs, especially together:

  • The sender address is not the real one. Look closely at the address behind the name. Real Amazon mail comes from amazon.com and real PayPal mail from paypal.com — not a close misspelling, not an extra word tacked on, and not a free email address. A name that says "Amazon" with a strange address behind it is fake.
  • It greets you like a stranger. "Dear customer" or "Dear user" instead of your name. Real Amazon and PayPal emails use the name on your account.
  • It is about an order or payment you do not recognize. A big order you never placed, a refund you never asked for, a payment "on hold." The shock is the point — it makes you click before you think.
  • It demands you act now. "Your account will be closed in 24 hours," "verify now or lose access," "confirm this payment or it will be charged." Real companies do not threaten you by email.
  • It asks you to log in, download, or pay through the email. A link to "sign in," an attachment to open, a request for your password, a code, a card number, or a gift card. Never sign in through an email link — go to the site or app yourself.
  • The details are slightly off. Odd spelling, strange wording, a logo that looks blurry or wrong, an order number that matches nothing in your account. One small wrong detail is enough to stop.

Brand impersonation — fake emails pretending to be a company you trust — is the most-reported scam category of all. Amazon and PayPal are two of the most impersonated names because almost everyone has an account with one of them.

What to do right now

  1. Do not click links and do not open attachments in the email. Not to "check" it, not to cancel the order — the link is the trap.
  2. Check it yourself another way. Open the Amazon or PayPal app on your phone, or type their address into your browser yourself, and look for the order, refund, or warning there. If it is not in your real account, the email is fake.
  3. Do not reply, and do not send personal details — no password, no code from your phone, no card number, no gift card codes.
  4. Report it, then delete it. Forward a fake Amazon email to stop-spoofing@amazon.com and a fake PayPal email to spoof@paypal.com, then delete it. You can also report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. If you already clicked and signed in, change your password from the real site right away and check your account for changes.
